# Daily Brief — Wednesday, 16 September 2026

## Top story: Fed's Kevin Warsh delivers his first rate decision this afternoon as the 10-year holds above 5% and Saudi Aramco starts cancelling European crude cargoes
The Federal Reserve's decision lands at 2pm ET (8pm CET) today, with Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference to follow at 2:30pm ET — the first rate call of his tenure since taking the gavel in May after the most divisive confirmation vote in Fed history (54-45). CME FedWatch odds sit between 85% and 92% for a 25bp hike to 3.75-4.00%, which would be the Fed's first increase since July 2023, up from roughly a coin-flip in late August before Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole remarks reset the market's expectations. The 10-year Treasury yield, which touched 5.04% intraday on Tuesday before closing near 5.01%, is holding just under the 5% line again this morning — the closely watched threshold it breached for the first time since 2007 earlier this week — reflecting how thoroughly a hike is already priced in; the bigger swing factor for markets today is less the decision itself than the dot plot and whether Warsh signals more hikes to come, with TD Securities already penciling in moves in October and January.

The oil-supply story that's been driving the inflation numbers behind this hike got more concrete overnight: Saudi Aramco has begun cancelling or delaying September crude cargoes to at least three European refineries, pushing some deliveries out to November, as the East-West pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz remains offline after last week's drone strikes. Poland's Orlen and other buyers are scrambling for alternatives, and physical cargo prices for replacement barrels have reportedly topped $120/bbl even as the Brent benchmark trades closer to $106-107 — a sign the disruption is biting harder in the physical market than the futures price alone suggests. Benchmark crude has actually eased slightly from Monday's highs (Brent near $106-107, WTI near $104.68, both down modestly on the day) as traders wait to see whether the Fed's tone today reinforces or complicates the inflation picture that oil has been feeding.

## Stock market & economy
Tuesday's cash session extended the week's losses: the S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,585.73, the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.78% to 25,981.57, and the Dow lost 328 points (0.63%) to 52,093.11, with rising Treasury yields doing most of the damage alongside continued, if somewhat calmer, pressure on chip stocks following Monday's AI-pacing selloff. Gold slipped further to around $4,317/oz, its lowest level in more than a month, as the hawkish-Fed narrative continues to outweigh the safe-haven bid that would normally accompany both a widening Gulf conflict and a wobbly equity market. Asian markets were subdued overnight awaiting the Fed outcome — the Nikkei sat roughly flat near 63,415, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng extended its slide to a fresh two-month low (24,667.24, -1.0% Tuesday) on weakness in HSBC and CATL — and European indices closed modestly lower Tuesday (Euro Stoxx 50 -0.4% to 6,236, AEX -0.23% to 1,095.87).

For the unhedged USD sleeve (IWDA, VUSA, VHYL), today's decision and dot plot are the single most consequential event of the month: a hike is largely priced in, so the market reaction will hinge on the forward guidance, and a hawkish surprise would likely extend both the dollar's strength and the pressure on the mega-cap tech names those funds are heaviest in; VHYL's value and energy tilt remains the natural offset if oil stays elevated. For IAEX and EUEA, Shell's leverage to firmer crude is the direct read-through, while a Fed hike would tend to support the dollar against the euro — a modest tailwind for the unhedged funds' EUR-translated returns and a modest headwind for European exporters more broadly.

## Off the beaten path: what a Kevin Warsh Fed actually means beyond today's headline hike
Today's rate decision is being covered as a single up-or-down call, but the more consequential story is the shift in how the Fed itself is run. Warsh took over in May after a 54-45 Senate vote — the narrowest and most contentious confirmation in the central bank's history — and has spent his first four months in office pushing the institution's reaction function markedly more hawkish than his predecessor's, culminating in Jackson Hole remarks that alone moved hike odds from roughly a coin-flip to near-certain within days. That's a structural change in how monetary policy gets made, not a one-meeting event: if today's dot plot confirms the October/January hike path some desks are already pricing, it would mark the first sustained hiking cycle since 2022-2023 and a genuine regime change for rate-sensitive assets after nearly three years without an increase.

The read-through for a diversified portfolio is more about volatility than direction — a more hawkish, less predictable Fed chair raises the odds of larger single-day moves around each future meeting, which is exactly what's been happening to the 10-year yield and mega-cap tech valuations over the past three weeks. None of IWDA, VUSA, VHYL, IAEX or EUEA can be repositioned around a single official's reaction function, and Warsh's own framework could shift again once the current oil-driven inflation spike fades — this is a "watch how the dot plot evolves over the next two or three meetings" story, not a reason to change anything today.
