# Daily Brief — Wednesday, 2 September 2026

## Top story: bond markets, not equities, are driving the story
The past 24 hours were less about a single headline and more about a global bond sell-off that's now pressuring stocks everywhere. US 30-year yields are at their worst run since 2006, the 10-year hit 4.75-4.79% (highest since January 2025), and — more strikingly — Japan's 10-year yield touched 3% on Monday for the first time since 1996. Add in the still-live US-Iran confrontation near the Strait of Hormuz (oil back above $87-90 after fresh US strikes on Iranian positions on Larak Island), and you have inflation-fear, rate-fear and geopolitical-fear all pulling the same direction at once. US equities slid Monday on the combination: Nasdaq -0.9%, S&P 500 -0.68%, Dow -0.79%, with tech and industrials underperforming and defensives holding up better. The VIX rose to 15.8.

## Stock market & economy
- **Rates:** CME FedWatch now prices September 16 Fed hike odds at 65-68%, more than double the ~36% seen before Chair Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole speech. Driving it: 12-month PCE at 3.7% (nearly double target), 6-month annualized PCE at 4.1%, and now an oil-driven inflation impulse from the Iran conflict.
- **Data out this week:** August ISM Manufacturing came in at 54.6 (down from 55.6, still expansionary) but the Prices Paid sub-index jumped to 71.1 — a level that has historically preceded goods-inflation showing up in producer/consumer prices within 45-60 days. JOLTS showed openings falling to 7.27M and the quits rate slipping to 1.9% (below the 2.0% line that typically signals a cooling labor market). The big one is Friday: August nonfarm payrolls, following July's shock -23,000 print. August CPI lands around September 10, the last data point before the September 16-17 FOMC meeting and blackout period.
- **Commodities:** Brent/WTI both pushing back toward $90 on Hormuz risk; about a fifth of global oil trade transits the strait. Gold sits around $4,420-4,450, off its recent highs but still up sharply over the past month as a safe-haven hedge.
- **Japan/global spillover:** The BOJ is expected (80-90% priced in) to hike to 1.25% at its September 17-18 meeting — a policy rate last seen 31 years ago. Yen has weakened toward 160/dollar, near levels that have triggered intervention before; Treasury Secretary Bessent commented at the G20 that markets are "pricing in" further tightening. This matters for Ruben's IWDA/VUSA/VHYL exposure indirectly through global bond-yield correlation, and directly through IWDA's ~5-6% Japan weighting.
- **Earnings this week:** Broadcom, Dell, Palo Alto Networks, MongoDB, Snowflake, HPE and lululemon all report, testing whether AI/tech valuations can hold up against the rising-yield backdrop.
- **Bottom line:** three reinforcing pressures — a probable Fed hike, a possible BOJ hike, and an active Middle East conflict pushing oil up — are all pointing the same direction (higher rates, higher inflation risk), which is unusual and is why bonds are leading equities lower rather than the other way around.

## Off the beaten path: Japan's rate-normalization trade
The headline reaction to Japan's 10-year yield hitting 3% has mostly been "bond sell-off, risk-off." But the flip side is a genuine equity opportunity: Japanese megabanks are posting record profits as 30 years of near-zero rates finally end. MUFG, SMFG and Mizuho have all reported record results as lending spreads widen, and MUFG is combining that with active buybacks and a portfolio shift toward higher-return assets. Rakuten Bank is a smaller, higher-beta way to play the same theme — flagged by analysts as trading well below fair-value estimates and benefiting disproportionately from margin expansion as a digital-first lender.

The risk side is real: SMFG's ROE is still a modest ~6.5%, MUFG's re-positioning carries execution risk, and a sharper-than-expected yield spike can hit banks' existing bond holdings before it helps their new lending margins. It's also a currency call — further BOJ tightening could stabilize or strengthen the yen after its recent slide toward 160, which would help euro-based returns on top of the equity story. Unlike the tanker/freight trade flagged in yesterday's brief, this one isn't tied to how the Iran conflict resolves — it's a structural, multi-year unwind of Japan's zero-rate era that's just now showing up in bank earnings, making it a genuinely different, less crowded angle than the usual oil and defense plays.
