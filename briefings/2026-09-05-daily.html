Daily briefSaturday, 5 September 2026

Jobs blow past forecasts, killing near-term rate-cut hopes just as the ECB gets set to hike

Friday's August nonfarm payrolls report landed nowhere near where markets were braced: the US added 162,000 jobs versus a consensus of roughly 53,000–55,000, and unemployment held at 4.1%. After July's contraction and a soft ADP print, this was shaping up as the print that tipped the Fed toward a September cut — instead it did the opposite. Equities took it in stride: the S&P 500 dipped just 0.08% to 7,741.86 and the Dow fell 0.33% to 53,509.14, while the Nasdaq rose 0.12% to 26,615.08. Gold sold off harder, down 1.6% to $4,468.60, and oil eased too (WTI –2.3% to $89.24) even as the Iran conflict ground on. A Fed decision that looked close to a coin flip now tilts toward "hold, and possibly hawkish."

Stock market & economy

Broadcom capped the week's earnings run with a blowout print: Q3 revenue of $29.59bn beat estimates, AI semiconductor revenue hit $16.7bn (+221% y/y) and now makes up 70% of total revenue, with guidance calling for AI chip revenue to accelerate to $21.7bn in Q4. Combined with Dell, GitLab and Snowflake's strong prints, the picture across AI infrastructure and enterprise software demand remains firm.

The Iran war (day 189 as of Friday) has settled into an uneasy, lower-intensity pattern: Iran struck Kuwaiti and UAE targets on the night of September 3rd, the US hadn't struck back in two days, and VP Vance downgraded the rhetoric to "no active shooting." Brent touched $97 on September 3rd before easing to about $95, tracking an 8% weekly gain. Strait of Hormuz transit counts diverge wildly by source, but every measure agrees flow is a fraction of normal. US gasoline is now averaging $4.14/gallon and diesel sits at four-year highs.

The next dated catalyst is the ECB's September 10 decision: a 25bp hike to a 2.50% deposit rate is essentially consensus given 3.3% inflation, but the real question — likely to move the AEX index and EURO STOXX 50 ETFs — is whether Lagarde leans "mildly restrictive" or pushes back on market pricing implying a 3.00% peak in 2027.

Also in the news

European natural gas storage sits at roughly 63–65% of capacity, the lowest for early September in almost two decades. The Netherlands has warned it may miss its own storage target, and ING analysts say even the EU's lowered 75% November 1 goal looks hard to hit — a mix of a hot summer refill season and the Iran war cutting off Qatari LNG that would normally flow to Europe. Dutch TTF futures have pushed to their highest levels since 2023.

Off the beaten path — LNG shipping's spot-rate spike

LNG carrier spot charter rates are at what brokers call "unthinkable levels," with Atlantic-basin rates reported as high as $300,000/day, the strongest since early 2024, as Qatari and UAE cargoes increasingly move via ship-to-ship transfers to dodge Hormuz risk. Flex LNG (NYSE: FLNG, ~9.5% dividend yield) is the most liquid pure-play, though management has kept most 2026 days on fixed-rate contracts, so it only partially captures a spike like this. If the Iran situation cools or Europe's restocking need eases, this reverses quickly — a live, weather- and geopolitics-dependent trade, not a structural one.
