Daily brief Saturday, 5 September 2026 · 4 min read

Jobs blow past forecasts, killing near-term rate-cut hopes just as the ECB gets set to hike

Friday's August nonfarm payrolls report landed nowhere near where markets were braced for it: the US added 162,000 jobs versus a consensus of roughly 53,000-55,000, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%. After July's contraction and a soft ADP print earlier in the week, this was shaping up as the print that tipped the Fed toward a September 16-17 cut — instead it does the opposite. Fifth Third's Bill Adams summed up the shift: the report "was much better than expected, focusing the Fed squarely on controlling inflation when they meet next in September," reversing the dovish lean that had been building. Equities took it in stride rather than panicking: the S&P 500 dipped just 0.08% to 7,741.86 and the Dow fell 0.33% to 53,509.14, while the Nasdaq actually rose 0.12% to 26,615.08. Gold sold off harder, down 1.6% to $4,468.60, and oil eased too (WTI -2.3% to $89.24) even as the Iran conflict ground on. The net effect: a Fed decision that looked close to a coin flip a few days ago now tilts toward "hold, and possibly hawkish" — an unhelpful backdrop for anyone hoping rate relief was close.

02Stock market & economy

Broadcom capped the week's earnings run with a blowout Wednesday print: Q3 revenue of $29.59bn beat estimates, AI semiconductor revenue hit $16.7bn (+221% y/y) and now makes up 70% of total revenue, and guidance calls for AI chip revenue to accelerate further to $21.7bn in Q4 (+236% y/y). CEO Hock Tan also unveiled VMware-based private-AI cloud platforms, pushing Broadcom further into enterprise infrastructure alongside its hyperscaler chip business. Combined with Dell, GitLab and Snowflake's strong prints earlier in the week, the picture across AI infrastructure and enterprise software demand remains firm even as the broader "AI disruption" narrative around SaaS lingers in the background.

The Iran war (day 189 as of Friday) has settled into an uneasy, lower-intensity pattern rather than resolving: Iran struck Kuwaiti and UAE targets on the night of September 3rd, the US hasn't struck back in two days, and VP Vance downgraded the rhetoric to "no active shooting," while still conditioning any talks on Iran halting attacks on commercial shipping. Brent touched $97 on September 3rd (highest since July 23) before easing to about $95, tracking an 8% weekly gain. Strait of Hormuz transit counts diverge wildly by source — CENTCOM says 87 vessels have been redirected since mid-July, while commercial tracker Kpler counted just 6 transits on September 2nd against a 130+/day pre-war baseline — but every measure agrees flow is a fraction of normal. US gasoline is now averaging $4.14/gallon and US diesel sits at four-year highs.

The next dated catalyst is the ECB's September 10 decision: a 25bp hike to a 2.50% deposit rate is essentially consensus given August eurozone inflation at 3.3%, but the real question — and the one likely to move EUR-denominated holdings (AEX index ETF, EURO STOXX 50 ETF) — is whether Lagarde leans toward the "mildly restrictive" camp some Governing Council members want or pushes back on market pricing that implies a 3.00% peak in 2027, which analysts view as excessive. A hawkish tilt on guidance, more than the hike itself, is the real risk event here.

Bottom line for the portfolio: the Fed's September decision just got harder to call in the dovish direction, the ECB's hike is priced but its guidance isn't, and both land within the next two weeks — a stretch where USD and EUR legs of the portfolio could easily diverge on rate-path repricing alone.

03Also in the news

European natural gas storage is the story getting less attention than the US jobs number but arguably carries more direct relevance for a euro-based portfolio: storage sits at roughly 63-65% of capacity, the lowest for early September in almost two decades, well below the five-year average. The Netherlands has already warned it may miss its own storage target, and ING analysts say even the EU's lowered 75% November 1 goal looks hard to hit. The cause is a mix of a hot summer refill season and the Iran war cutting off a chunk of Qatari LNG that would normally flow to Europe but is now stuck behind Hormuz delays. Dutch TTF futures have pushed to their highest levels since 2023, and the consensus risk case is materially higher household energy bills across Europe this winter if a cold snap coincides with the shortfall.

04Off the beaten path

LNG shipping's spot-rate spike as a play on Europe's storage shortfall

The obvious Hormuz trades — tanker owners, marine war-risk insurers, air-freight handlers — have all been flagged in this brief already and are increasingly consensus. A layer that's had less attention: LNG carrier spot charter rates, which brokers now describe as being at "unthinkable levels," with Atlantic-basin rates reported as high as $300,000/day, the strongest since early 2024. The logic is straightforward — Qatari and UAE cargoes are increasingly moving via rare ship-to-ship transfers outside the Strait to dodge Hormuz risk, which eats up vessel-days and tightens available Atlantic tonnage right as Europe needs every LNG cargo it can get to defend its storage target.

The pure-play names are smaller, higher-beta LNG shipowners rather than the oil majors: Flex LNG (NYSE: FLNG, 13-vessel fleet, ~9.5% dividend yield) is the most liquid, though it's worth noting that management has kept the bulk of 2026 days on fixed-rate contracts rather than pure spot exposure — good for earnings stability, but it means FLNG itself only partially captures a spot-rate spike like this one. That's the honest caveat with this whole idea: the shipowners with genuine spot exposure benefit most, but they're also the ones most exposed if the rate collapses just as fast, which is exactly what happened the last time Atlantic LNG rates spiked and then fell back through 2025 as the market normalized. If the Iran situation cools or Europe's restocking need eases, this reverses quickly — it's a live, weather- and geopolitics-dependent trade, not a structural one. Worth tracking rather than chasing at $300k/day print highs.
