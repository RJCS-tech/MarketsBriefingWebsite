Weekly Markets Report — 14–20 September 2026

This was the week the Fed finally moved, and markets spent five days digesting exactly how hawkish that move was. The FOMC delivered its widely-priced 25bp hike on Wednesday 16 September, taking the funds rate to 3.75-4.00% in a unanimous 12-0 decision — the first increase since July 2023. Stocks initially rallied into the 2pm decision, then reversed hard once new Chair Kevin Warsh opened his press conference: he said he'd be "hard pressed to describe broad financial conditions as restrictive," and the updated dot plot showed most officials penciling in at least one more hike this year, sending the Dow down 631 points (-1.21%) on the day and pushing the 10-year Treasury yield to 5.04% intraday, its first breach of 5% since 2007. Thursday brought a sharp reversal (Dow +316pts, Nasdaq +1.69%) as Saudi Arabia eased the oil-supply squeeze by routing extra crude to Asian refiners via ship-to-ship transfers near Oman's Sohar port, and Friday closed the week little-changed. Net for the week: the S&P 500 finished essentially flat (~7,650.50), the Nasdaq gained on AI-adjacent strength (~26,522.55), and the Dow logged its third straight losing week (~51,682.64) — its worst since March. The Bank of Japan added a second tightening pulse Friday, hiking to 1.25% (highest since 1995) in a split 7-2 vote, though two dissents left traders doubting further moves and the yen fell anyway. The week's other major shock came Monday, when an Anthropic CEO essay over the weekend calling for AI developers to slow down triggered a broad chip-stock selloff (Nvidia -3.4%, the PHLX semiconductor index -6%, its worst day since July) and pushed OpenAI to delay its IPO to 2027, while Anthropic itself pressed ahead with a planned Nasdaq listing this fall. On the geopolitical side, Trump signed the Graham Russia-Iran sanctions bill into law Friday, authorizing tariffs of up to 100% on countries that keep buying Russian oil and gas — a significant escalation of the economic-pressure track even as the shooting war grinds on with no breakthrough (an October UAE trilateral is proposed but undated). The Gulf war remains unresolved and, if anything, more entrenched: Iran's parliament speaker said Sunday that the Strait of Hormuz stays closed until Tehran's conditions are met, IMF PortWatch data show transits have collapsed to roughly 8 a day against an 85-a-day pre-crisis baseline, and US diesel hit a fresh record above $6.40/gallon.

03Portfolio implicationsRuben's ETFs: AEX index ETF, EURO STOXX 50 ETF, MSCI World ETF, S&P 500 ETF, High Dividend Yield ETF

  • The Fed decision is now behind us, but Warsh's press conference reopened rather than closed the rate question for the unhedged USD sleeve (MSCI World ETF, S&P 500 ETF, High Dividend Yield ETF): prediction markets already put the odds of a second hike at the 28 October meeting at roughly 55-70%, and the 10-year's break above 5% (a level not seen since 2007) is a genuine regime shift in the discount rate applied to the mega-cap growth names these funds are heaviest in.
  • A second, distinct volatility source opened this week alongside rates: the AI-slowdown debate sparked by Amodei's essay hit chip stocks broadly (Nvidia, Broadcom, Intel, Marvell all down sharply Monday) before Thursday's rebound clawed most of it back — a reminder that MSCI World ETF/S&P 500 ETF's tech concentration is now exposed to safety-and-governance headlines, not just yield moves, as both OpenAI's delayed IPO and Anthropic's planned Nasdaq listing keep the theme in the news this autumn.
  • High Dividend Yield ETF's value/energy/financials tilt remains the natural offset to elevated oil, though the tailwind is more muted than last week now that Brent has pulled back off its post-Jordan-strike highs; a genuinely closed Hormuz and Iran's hardened "no reopening" stance mean the floor under oil prices looks sturdy even as the ceiling eases.
  • Trump's newly signed Russia-Iran sanctions bill (up to 100% tariffs on countries still buying Russian energy) is a fresh, underappreciated risk that cuts across all five holdings: it targets trade flows well beyond Russia itself and could disrupt global energy and goods markets in ways that feed straight back into the same inflation and yield pressures already driving this year's volatility.
  • AEX index ETF and EURO STOXX 50 ETF (EUR-denominated, no direct FX effect): a third central bank (the BOJ) joined the Fed and ECB in active tightening this week, reinforcing that developed-market monetary policy is now synchronized in a way it hasn't been in years — a headwind for equity multiples generally, though Shell's leverage to firmer-for-longer oil remains a partial offset within AEX index ETF specifically.
  • Currency: the dollar index rose 0.6% on the Fed's hawkish tone, a tailwind for EUR-translated returns on the unhedged sleeve this week, but the yen's decline despite a BOJ hike shows FX is trading on policy-path credibility as much as on the headline rate move — worth watching as a template for how EUR/USD could react to the October Fed meeting.
