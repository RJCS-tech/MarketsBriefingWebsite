Daily briefWednesday, 2 September 2026

Bond markets, not equities, are driving the story

The past 24 hours were less about a single headline and more about a global bond sell-off now pressuring stocks everywhere. US 30-year yields are on their worst run since 2006, the 10-year hit 4.75–4.79% (highest since January 2025), and Japan's 10-year touched 3% for the first time since 1996. Add the still-live US–Iran confrontation near Hormuz — oil back above $87–90 after fresh US strikes on Larak Island — and inflation-fear, rate-fear and geopolitical-fear are all pulling the same direction at once. US equities slid Monday: Nasdaq –0.9%, S&P 500 –0.68%, Dow –0.79%, with tech and industrials underperforming and defensives holding up better. The VIX rose to 15.8.

Stock market & economy

Rates: CME FedWatch prices September 16 hike odds at 65–68%, more than double the ~36% seen before Warsh's Jackson Hole speech — driven by 12-month PCE at 3.7%, 6-month annualized PCE at 4.1%, and now an oil-driven inflation impulse from Iran.

Data: August ISM Manufacturing came in at 54.6 (down from 55.6, still expansionary), but the Prices Paid sub-index jumped to 71.1 — a level that has historically preceded goods-inflation showing up within 45–60 days. JOLTS openings fell to 7.27M and quits slipped to 1.9%, below the line that typically signals a cooling labor market. Friday's August payrolls follow July's shock –23,000 print; August CPI lands around September 10, the last data point before the FOMC blackout.

Commodities: Brent/WTI both pushing back toward $90 on Hormuz risk. Gold around $4,420–4,450, off its highs but still up sharply over the past month.

Japan/global spillover: the BOJ is 80–90% priced to hike to 1.25% at its September 17–18 meeting, a rate last seen 31 years ago. The yen has weakened toward 160/dollar, near levels that have triggered intervention before. This matters for Ruben's MSCI World, S&P 500 and High Dividend Yield ETF exposure via global bond-yield correlation and directly through the MSCI World ETF's ~5–6% Japan weighting.

Earnings this week: Broadcom, Dell, Palo Alto Networks, MongoDB, Snowflake, HPE and lululemon all report, testing whether AI/tech valuations can hold against rising yields.

Also in the news

Gaza's ceasefire remains fragile per ongoing reporting, though no fresh escalation was confirmed in the last 24 hours. Trump has signaled further retaliation is likely if Iran responds to the Larak Island strikes; markets are treating this as active, not resolved.

Off the beaten path — Japan's rate-normalization trade

Japanese megabanks are posting record profits as 30 years of near-zero rates end. MUFG, SMFG and Mizuho have all reported record results as lending spreads widen; Rakuten Bank is a smaller, higher-beta way to play the theme. The risk side is real — SMFG's ROE is still a modest ~6.5%, and a sharper-than-expected yield spike can hit banks' existing bond holdings before it helps new lending margins. It's also a currency call: further BOJ tightening could stabilize the yen, helping euro-based returns on top of the equity story.
