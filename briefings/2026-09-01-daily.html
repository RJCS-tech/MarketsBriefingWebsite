Daily briefTuesday, 1 September 2026

US–Iran fighting flares again, oil and rate-hike fears back on the table

Over the long weekend, US and Iranian forces exchanged fire for the first time in about a month. A tanker was struck by three projectiles off Oman's coast in the Strait of Hormuz, and US forces hit Iranian rocket-launcher positions on Larak Island on Sunday. Trump said the US would retaliate hard, while Treasury Secretary Bessent framed Iran's actions as a sign of economic desperation, pointing to fuel shortages and long gas lines inside the country despite its own energy reserves. It sits atop a war that's run on and off since a US "major combat operations" announcement earlier this year, with a June ceasefire attempt that never fully held.

Markets reacted immediately: oil jumped 3.4% on the news, and with Tuesday the first US session after Labor Day, futures were down across the board — S&P 500 –0.6%, Dow –0.5%, Nasdaq –1.0% — as investors priced in both the geopolitical risk and its inflationary knock-on effects.

Stock market & economy

Futures/pre-open: S&P 500 ~7,656 (–0.6%), Dow ~52,960 (–0.5%), Nasdaq-100 ~29,213 (–1.0%). August closed with solid gains, but September is seasonally the market's weakest month, and this year it opened under a geopolitical cloud.

Rates: the US 10-year climbed to 4.75%. The September Fed decision is being called "a coin flip" — hike odds have risen since Warsh's hawkish Jackson Hole tone, and the Iran-driven oil spike adds another inflationary push favoring a hike over a cut.

Commodities: Brent near $90/bbl on Hormuz risk (about a fifth of global oil trade transits the strait). Gold around $4,453/oz — down about 3% on the week but still up roughly 10% over the past month.

Data to watch: ISM and S&P Global manufacturing PMIs, construction spending and JOLTS today; August jobs report Friday. Earnings this week include Palo Alto Networks, Dell, Medtronic and MongoDB.

Also in the news

  • Gaza: the ceasefire remains fragile. The IDF killed at least five people in Gaza City strikes Monday, including on a displacement encampment; the cumulative death toll since the ceasefire began has reportedly passed 1,300.
  • Rare earths: with the Iran conflict disrupting parts of the supply chain, several rare-earth names are getting fresh attention as a hedge against further Middle East-linked disruption.

Off the beaten path — tanker freight and war-risk insurance

VLCC freight rates on the Gulf-to-China route jumped 24% in a single day — the steepest one-day spike this year — as owners reroute around the strait and insurers layer on war-risk premiums, reportedly adding $1.5–4.5m per voyage. Tanker owners with unhedged spot exposure — Frontline, Scorpio Tankers, Teekay Tankers — have been cited by analysts as beneficiaries. It's a higher-beta, event-driven trade rather than a core holding: freight rates can reverse just as fast if a ceasefire sticks.
