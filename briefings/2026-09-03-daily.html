Daily briefThursday, 3 September 2026

Oil back above $95, tanker traffic through Hormuz collapsing

Fresh US–Iran fighting escalated overnight: Trump announced "large and powerful" strikes on Iranian positions after Iran allegedly tried to plant sea mines and struck a US base in Jordan; Iran has pledged retaliation, and Kuwait, Jordan and Bahrain intercepted missiles Wednesday morning. Brent jumped to $95.47 and WTI to $90.72 — both benchmarks' strongest one-day move in over a month — before easing to $89.71 as some panic faded. The more telling number is traffic: vessel crossings through Hormuz fell from 23 the previous Wednesday to just 10 on Monday and 5 on Tuesday, a fifth of normal flow through a route carrying roughly a fifth of the world's seaborne oil. US equities still firmed — Dow +0.53%, S&P 500 +0.20%, Nasdaq +0.03%, Russell 2000 +0.75% — even as the 10-year yield pushed to 4.81%, its highest since November 2023.

Stock market & economy

Snowflake blew past estimates after Wednesday's close: Q2 revenue $1.55bn (+35% y/y) versus $1.48bn expected, adjusted EPS $0.62 versus $0.45 expected, and raised full-year guidance to $6.1bn product revenue with operating margin lifted to 14.5%. The stock jumped over 22% in extended trading — a strong signal enterprise AI-infrastructure demand is intact despite the "AI agents will disrupt SaaS" narrative.

Labor data continues to soften: ADP private payrolls rose just 38,000 in August, below the 47,000 estimate and the weakest print since January. That sets up Friday's official payrolls as the single most important data point before the September FOMC meeting; August CPI on September 10 is the other major input. Fed hike odds are now essentially a coin flip. Gold, which would normally catch a safe-haven bid on this kind of escalation, instead drifted down to $4,357–4,377/oz, because traders are reading "higher oil → higher odds of a Fed hike" as the dominant force — a useful tell on how markets are weighing geopolitical fear against rate fear.

Also in the news

Gaza's ceasefire remains fragile and diplomatically contested — China and Egypt jointly called for a "truly lasting" ceasefire at a Cairo meeting this week. No fresh military escalation was reported there in the last 24 hours, but it stays on the watch list alongside the more acute US–Iran situation, which is what's actually moving oil and yields right now. China–Taiwan tensions remain elevated in the background but produced no new headlines overnight.

Off the beaten path — Lloyd's specialty insurers riding the war-risk premium spike

A less obvious beneficiary sits one layer removed from tankers: the Lloyd's specialty and marine war-risk insurers who underwrite cover for the Gulf. War-risk premiums for a single Hormuz transit have reportedly climbed into the double-digit millions; Beazley announced a $1bn Marine War consortium in April. Lancashire Holdings (LSE: LRE) posted a strong H1 2026 with a 90.8% combined ratio. The appeal: insurers collect premium income regardless of whether ships ultimately sail. The risk: tail risk if a major vessel loss occurs, and any resolution of the Iran conflict would compress these premiums quickly. Both LRE and Beazley are GBP-denominated — a currency dimension for a euro-based portfolio.
