Weekly report24–30 August 2026

Warsh turns hawkish at Jackson Hole, Nvidia keeps the AI trade alive

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first major Jackson Hole speech called inflation "concerning" and said the Fed "has more work to do," flipping market expectations from rate cuts toward a real chance of a September hike — implied odds jumped from roughly 35% to 57%. Nvidia posted a blowout Q2 FY2027 (revenue $96.2bn, +106% y/y; guided 70% FY2028 growth; stock +9%), keeping AI and tech risk appetite alive even as rate-sensitive growth names wobbled late in the week.

Eurozone flash PMIs hit a nine-month high, led by Germany, but inflation stayed sticky — the ECB's Lane flagged roughly 3% through year-end. Geopolitically: a US–Canada tariff deal reduced trade risk, the Gaza ceasefire was called a "point of no return" risk by the US envoy, the Russia–Ukraine war remained unresolved, and China–Taiwan tensions kept escalating (a new PRC naval base, a record Taiwan defense budget). Oil sat around $88–94/bbl, gold was firm, and the dollar rallied after Warsh's remarks, pulling EUR/USD back from its ~1.1676 high.

Portfolio implications

  • Currency: the MSCI World, S&P 500 and High Dividend Yield ETFs are unhedged USD — the dollar rally was a EUR-value tailwind for roughly 60% of the portfolio, though this reverses if the hawkish narrative fades.
  • Rate sensitivity: the MSCI World and S&P 500 ETFs (mega-cap tech heavy) are most exposed to hawkish Fed repricing; the High Dividend Yield ETF (dividend/value, financials and energy tilt) is most insulated and benefits from higher-for-longer rates and firm oil.
  • The AEX index and EURO STOXX 50 ETFs (EUR-denominated, no FX effect): supported by strong eurozone PMIs and German manufacturing, but capped by sticky inflation and a cautious ECB. The AEX index ETF carries direct ASML/chip-cycle and Shell/oil-price exposure.
  • Geopolitical tail risk (Gaza, Russia–Ukraine, China–Taiwan) isn't priced aggressively into equities yet, but is a plausible risk-off trigger via oil and semiconductor supply chains — touching several holdings at once.
