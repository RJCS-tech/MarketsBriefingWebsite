Daily briefFriday, 4 September 2026

Jobs day arrives with the Fed on a knife-edge, and the Iran war keeps widening

Today brings the single most-watched data point before the September 16–17 FOMC meeting: August nonfarm payrolls, due at 8:30am ET, following July's shock contraction and Wednesday's weak ADP print. Consensus clusters around +53,000, but after two straight downside surprises the bar is arguably lower than the headline suggests — another weak print would likely tip the Fed back toward a cut, while a solid beat plus this week's oil-driven inflation impulse keeps a hike in play. Layered on top: the US–Iran conflict escalated further overnight, with Iran's Health Ministry reporting 105 civilians injured near a wedding venue in Sirik after a US strike, and Tehran retaliating with missile and drone strikes intercepted by Kuwait, alongside strikes attributed to Iran against UAE targets. Markets shrugged off the geopolitics Wednesday — Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq all closed roughly 0.5–0.6% higher — while bond markets did not: the 10-year hit 4.82%, its highest since November 2023.

Stock market & economy

Wednesday's close: S&P 500 +0.5% to 7,666.60, Nasdaq +0.5% to 26,217.83, Dow +0.6% to 53,061.95. Earnings did the heavy lifting — Dell surged 15.8% on adjusted EPS of $7.04 versus $4.97 expected, GitLab jumped 10%, and Snowflake's blowout print is still rippling through enterprise software sentiment. Energy was the strongest sector (+1.3%) as WTI rose to $91–93 and Brent pushed above $97, while seven of eleven S&P sectors actually finished lower.

The bigger story may be in Europe: eurozone inflation jumped to 3.3% in August from 2.9% in July — the highest in nearly three years — driven almost entirely by energy, with ECB economists attributing roughly 90% of the recent energy-inflation increase to the Middle East supply shock. Markets are now pricing a near-certain ECB hike on September 10, taking the deposit rate from 2.25% to 2.50%. That's a live, dated catalyst for the AEX index and EURO STOXX 50 ETFs: a hike this soon after June suggests the ECB is genuinely worried about a second inflation wave. Gold, meanwhile, is trading around $4,470/oz — a shift from earlier in the week, when it had been falling on rising Fed-hike odds. That flip suggests the market's read on "which fear wins" is genuinely unsettled right now.

Also in the news

The Hormuz conflict is reshaping global trade flows well beyond oil tankers. Vessels are increasingly rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope — adding 10–14 sailing days — pushing sea freight costs on India-focused lanes up 30–50% and air cargo rates on India–Middle East routes up 250–300%. India's trade deficit has nearly doubled year-over-year to $27.1bn and the rupee has weakened to about 92.3/USD.

Off the beaten path — air cargo and freight-handling

A less obvious beneficiary of the disruption is the air-cargo and freight-handling layer absorbing shipments diverted off the sea route entirely. Singapore's SATS Ltd just reported record Q1 FY27 cargo volumes of 2.59 million tonnes (+8.6% y/y), with management explicitly citing "geopolitical rerouting." India's Blue Dart Express sits at the center of the India–Gulf air-freight squeeze, though no confirmed earnings read-through has surfaced yet. The risk: SATS itself flagged that the disruption is also compressing its EBITDA margins, and a real ceasefire would likely unwind the premium pricing fast.
