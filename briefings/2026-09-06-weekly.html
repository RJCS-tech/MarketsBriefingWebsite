Weekly report31 August – 6 September 2026

Iran escalates further as US envoys push Russia and Ukraine toward the table

Two live geopolitical fronts moved in opposite directions this week. In the Gulf, the US–Iran conflict escalated further: CENTCOM confirmed strikes on three Iranian government-linked oil tankers near Kharg Island and Jask on 5 September, under a new "tanker for tanker" doctrine, after Iranian strikes on US Navy vessels and continued attacks on Gulf shipping. Meanwhile, US envoys Witkoff and Kushner met Putin in Moscow then travelled to Kyiv — the highest-level US diplomatic push on Russia–Ukraine in six months — with a temporary ceasefire holding through 8 September.

On the macro side, the US August jobs report blew past expectations (162,000 vs. ~55,000 consensus), reversing rate-cut hopes and pushing the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.78–4.82%, its highest since November 2023; the Fed's September 16–17 meeting now looks closer to a hold/hawkish lean than the cut priced in a week earlier. Eurozone inflation jumped to 3.3% (highest in ~3 years, ~90% energy-driven), with a 25bp ECB hike to 2.50% on 10 September close to consensus — Lagarde's guidance tone, not the hike, is the real risk event. AI-infrastructure earnings stayed strong (Broadcom AI revenue +221% y/y; Nvidia's ~$13bn Hugging Face acquisition), helping offset — not fully cancel — the drag from rising yields on tech multiples. Oil spiked toward $95–97/bbl (Brent) before easing; gold fell ~1–2% as the hawkish repricing hit non-yielding assets; the dollar firmed (DXY ~99.2). European gas storage sits at its lowest for early September in almost two decades — a slower-moving euro-relevant risk into winter.

Portfolio implications

  • Currency: ~60% of the portfolio (the MSCI World, S&P 500 and High Dividend Yield ETFs) is unhedged USD — this week's dollar firming was a EUR-value tailwind, reversing if the hawkish-Fed narrative fades or the ECB surprises hawkish enough to support the euro.
  • Rate sensitivity: the MSCI World and S&P 500 ETFs are most exposed to Fed repricing but also the most direct beneficiaries of continued AI-capex momentum (Broadcom, Nvidia) — a genuine two-sided setup. The High Dividend Yield ETF is most insulated from rate-driven multiple compression and benefits from firmer oil.
  • The AEX index and EURO STOXX 50 ETFs: supported by firm eurozone PMIs, capped by sticky inflation and a hawkish-leaning ECB. Lagarde's 10 September guidance tone — not the hike itself — is the swing factor. The AEX index ETF carries added ASML/semiconductor-cycle and Shell/energy-price exposure.
  • Geopolitical tail risk: the Iran conflict and Europe's gas-storage shortfall are more direct channels into this portfolio (oil, energy costs, semiconductor supply chains) than the Russia–Ukraine story, which affects European risk premia more broadly. Neither conflict is resolved, and equities haven't yet had a full session to price the weekend's developments together.
